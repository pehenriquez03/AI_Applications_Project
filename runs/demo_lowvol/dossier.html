Narrative template

What the paper proposes

The paper (The Cross-Section of Volatility and Expected Returns) proposes Idiosyncratic volatility: the standard deviation of the residuals from a regression of each stock's daily returns on the Fama-French three factors (MKT, SMB, HML), estimated over the past one month of daily data as the signal, sorted by quintiles, favoring the lowest idiosyncratic volatility quintile (quintile 1). Rationale: The paper tests whether stocks with high idiosyncratic volatility relative to FF-3 have high exposure to aggregate volatility risk, which carries a negative price of risk; it finds this explains very little of the low returns and calls the pattern a puzzle. The result runs opposite to theories (e.g. Merton 1987) predicting a positive idiosyncratic volatility premium.

What we implemented and changed

We adapted 6 fields and assumed 3. Adapted: required_inputs (market capitalization used only for weighting, breakpoints or filters); breakpoints (no exchange or size breakpoints in our universe; equal-count sorts of eligible names); weighting (policy: equal weight everywhere); disfavored_group (policy: long-only, no short leg); universe_and_filters (policy: our 415-name S&P 500 survivor universe); sample_period (policy: our snapshot sample). Assumed: price_floor set to none; min_history_days set to 252; min_formation_days set to 15. Portfolios were formed in 132 months with 74.9 names on average.

What the backtest found

Over the sample the strategy returned 11.92% a year net of costs, against 12.71% for the equal-weight universe and 13.45% for SPY. Its Sharpe ratio was 0.63 against 0.65 for the equal-weight universe, and its maximum drawdown was -37.8%. The alpha t-statistic was -0.10; it is reported, not used as a gate.
