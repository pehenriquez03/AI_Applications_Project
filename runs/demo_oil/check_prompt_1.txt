Oil-Price Exposure as a Single-Factor Smart-Beta
Strategy
Mock research note | Teaching and API integration example | 27 October 2026
Status: This is a synthetic methodology note, not a published academic study. No empirical equity-return tests, performance estimates, or
statistically significant factor premia are asserted.
Abstract
This note proposes a single-factor equity strategy based on security-level sensitivity to changes in oil prices. Securities are
assigned an oil-price exposure (OILP) estimated from historical returns and oil-price changes. A constrained long-short
portfolio optimizer constructs positive-, zero-, and negative-OILP portfolios while controlling other modeled exposures. A
separate timing signal selects which portfolio to hold. The associated monthly oil-level series from the supplied OilPrices.csv
supports computation of oil changes and forward test targets; the series alone does not contain the security returns needed to
estimate stock exposures or execute the strategy.
1. Economic hypothesis and interpretation
Oil-price innovations can affect firms differently through production costs, revenues, demand conditions, and discount-rate
channels. We investigate whether an investable portfolio tilted toward firms with positive oil sensitivity performs differently
from a portfolio with negative oil sensitivity when oil prices change. The proposed single factor is oil-price sensitivity,
named OILP. A positive OILP portfolio has a positive portfolio beta to oil returns; a negative OILP portfolio has a negative
beta. The paper does not claim that high-OILP equities unconditionally earn higher average returns.
For an illustrative conditional hypothesis, higher contemporaneous or correctly forecast future oil returns favor positive oil
exposure, while lower oil returns favor negative exposure. This is a testable directional assumption, not a finding inferred from
the CSV.
2. Source data and definition
The provided CSV contains columns Date and Factor Level. We treat Factor Level as a monthly oil-related level P(t), without
assuming a specific crude benchmark, currency, inflation adjustment, or contract roll convention. Source coverage is January
2011–March 2024 (159 observations). Define monthly simple oil changes as r_oil(t) = P(t) / P(t-1) - 1. For interval forecasts,
define R_oil(t,T) = P(T) / P(t) - 1.

6. Portfolio construction methodology
On each chosen construction date, solve a constrained optimization across the available equity universe. In the existing
Strategy Economics implementation, a trailing cross-sectional regression yields historical residual returns e_i. The optimizer
seeks max_w sum_i w_i e_i, subject to target OILP exposure; targets for other included factors; preset gross long/short
allocations; name-level weight bounds; and a nonnegative fitted residual objective. Exact feasibility depends on the investable
universe and estimated exposures.
The selected equity portfolio must use the optimizer output with Symbol and Weight columns. Positive, neutral and negative
portfolios are not necessarily long-only: they are factor-exposure regimes formed through long and short positions.
7. Timing signal and backtest design
At each rebalance date t, a factor-timing signal s(t) is mapped into a regime: positive when s(t) is at least threshold h, negative
when s(t) is at most -h, and neutral otherwise. In the existing Monte Carlo exercise, signals of controlled predictive quality are
simulated from standardized future oil returns to measure the hypothetical economic value of forecast information. This is an
oracle-calibrated simulation, not a deployable forecast or a valid predictive trading backtest by itself.
Use monthly dates or explicitly defined holding intervals, compute realized stock returns only after the relevant decision
timestamp, and report turnover and transaction costs separately if they are introduced. A genuine investable experiment
would substitute a signal computed from information available at t.

8. Validation and evaluation
A valid factor-replication test should verify: (i) selected factor name equals OILP; (ii) positive/neutral/negative exposures
match user targets within optimizer tolerance; (iii) there are no duplicate names or missing return histories; (iv) the
rebalance-to-return alignment is correct; and (v) exposure estimates use appropriate historical information. Assess realized oil
beta, factor-neutrality residuals, turnover, Sharpe, drawdowns, and sensitivity to exposure targets.
9. Connection to fund economics
After constructing daily return paths, compare management and performance fee cash flows under a fixed fee schedule and
initial AUM. The marginal economic value of better oil forecasting is the discounted difference in fee cash flows between an
improved-information strategy and a matched zero-information baseline, less development and implementation costs. This
note supplies methodology and test inputs; it does not supply empirical cash-flow results.
10. Limitations
The dataset label does not identify which oil price or unit is represented. Simple level changes may differ from investable
futures returns because of contract rolls, carry and financing. Oil shocks can coincide with broader macroeconomic regimes,
and stock exposure estimates can be unstable. Static exposures and full-sample target normalization introduce look-ahead
risk. The regression-residual objective may favor historical noise; robustness requires rolling estimation, point-in-time data,
and true out-of-sample controls.
11. Explicit instructions for an LLM factor-extraction test
This note presents one clearly separable equity factor: OILP (sensitivity of security returns to oil-price movements). If an
available factor dictionary contains OILP: oil-price sensitivity of equity returns, the factor should be classified as
compatible. Factor direction should be interpreted carefully: the note proposes a conditional positive relation between oil
returns and the returns to positive-OILP exposure, but makes no unconditional expected-return-premium claim. All
numerical exposure targets are controlled by the implementation, not by the LLM.
