Deprioritize. The paper by Heston and Sadka reports that stocks which did well in the same calendar month in earlier years tend to do well in that month again, and the tested version sorts stocks into deciles on average returns at annual lags 24, 36, 48, and 60 months. Our long-only, equal-weighted test on a 415-name survivor universe over 2015 to 2025 returned a net CAGR of 8.03% against 12.71% for the equal-weighted universe, with a Sharpe of 0.38 against 0.65. The Monte Carlo fund economics are also negative: the probability that the net present value is positive is 12%, and the median incremental fee present value is -$3.0M against the equal-weighted universe fund. The main reason for the recommendation is that the strategy trails its benchmark, the edge fails at 10 bps of costs, and the decile ranking runs opposite to the paper's expected direction.

Narrative model-written

What the paper proposes

Heston and Sadka (2006), "Seasonality in the Cross-Section of Stock Returns", argue that individual stocks persistently earn different returns in specific calendar months, so a stock that did well in a given calendar month in past years tends to do well in that same month again. The experiment selected for this run is the Table 2 Years 2-5 annual strategy. Each month, stocks are ranked into equal-count deciles on the average of their returns at annual lags 24, 36, 48, and 60 months, portfolios are equal-weighted and held one month, and the paper reports a winner-minus-loser spread of 67 basis points per month with a t-statistic of 5.35 over January 1965 to December 2002. The expected sign is that the favored (highest) decile earns more than the lowest.

What we implemented and changed

We implemented the signal as the average of available annual-lag monthly returns at lags 24, 36, 48, and 60, ranked into 10 groups each month, equal-weighted, held one month, and rebalanced monthly. Execution uses the prior month's last close for the signal and the next month's first trading-day close for trades, with a cost of 10 bps. Four items were adapted: breakpoints are equal-count sorts over our eligible names rather than NYSE/AMEX stocks, the strategy is long-only so the paper's short leg is dropped, the universe is our 415-name S&P 500 survivor universe (19 names were excluded for missing Yahoo data), and the sample is 2015 to 2025 rather than 1965 to 2002. Five items were assumed because the paper is silent: no skip window, no price floor, a minimum of 2 available lags because the data snapshot starts in 2012 and the earliest formations have only lags 24 and 36, a history requirement of 252 days, and a formation-month minimum of 15 trading days. Eight details were left unresolved by the paper, including the return convention, delisting treatment, missing-return handling, and whether breakpoints use NYSE stocks only. The paper's text also conflicts with its own Table 2 on which decile is the winner, and we followed the table labeling.
