Narrative template

What the paper proposes

The paper ([MOCK] Maxing Out: Stocks as Lotteries and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns) proposes maximum daily return within the previous month as the signal, sorted by deciles, favoring the lowest MAX decile. Rationale: investors overpay for lottery-like stocks with extreme positive daily returns.

What we implemented and changed

We adapted 3 fields and assumed 2. Adapted: weighting (engine supports equal weighting only); strategy_portfolio (long-only adaptation; no short leg); universe_and_filters (snapshot universe). Assumed: price_floor set to none; min_formation_days set to 15. Portfolios were formed in 131 months with 35.0 names on average.

What the backtest found

Over the sample the strategy returned 3.98% a year net of costs, against 3.97% for the equal-weight universe and 3.62% for SPY. Its Sharpe ratio was 0.28 against 0.23 for the equal-weight universe, and its maximum drawdown was -39.1%. The alpha t-statistic was 0.32; it is reported, not used as a gate. Robustness across costs, sub-periods and ranked groups gave a rho of -0.92.

What the simulation and economics found

Across the simulated paths, incremental fee income versus the equal-weight universe fund had a median present value of $716k, with a 5th to 95th percentile range of -$1.4M to $2.7M. The share of paths with positive NPV after research cost was 71.4%, and 49.8% once the historical excess return is removed. Break-even AUM is $2.8M; it is reported, not headlined.

How reliable the interpretation was

Across 3 extraction runs the material fields agreed on average 95.8% of the time, and evidence was valid for 93.8% of material fields with 1 check-pass disagreements. Interpretation accuracy is scored against a preliminary reference.
